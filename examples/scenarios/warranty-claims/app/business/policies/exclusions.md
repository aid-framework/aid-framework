# Exclusions

## Wear items

Brake pads, brake discs, clutch friction surfaces, wiper blades, bulbs, tyres and filters are
excluded from warranty coverage. Wear is a consumable, not a defect, and the exclusion holds
even where the wear is faster than the owner expected.

A wear item that failed *because* a covered component failed is covered as part of that repair.
A seized caliper that destroyed a set of pads is a covered caliper and covered pads.

## Consequential damage

Damage to a component that follows from a failure in a different, excluded or expired component
is not covered. A water pump that fails after the base warranty expires and strips a timing belt
does not bring the cylinder head into coverage: the pump is the cause, the head is the
consequence, and neither is covered once the base warranty has run.

This exclusion is the most frequently disputed clause in the corpus. The test is causation, not
proximity: if the failed component is covered, its consequences are covered; if it is not, they
are not.

## Owner modification and misuse

Modifications that alter a covered system exclude that system. Reported symptoms that the
build record contradicts — a component reported as original that the service history shows
replaced, an odometer that runs backwards — are escalated rather than denied, because a denial
on those grounds is a fraud finding and a fraud finding is not an adjudicator's to make.

## Diagnostic-only visits

A visit that produces no repair is not a warranty claim. If the reported symptom could not be
reproduced, the correct determination is `deny` with a request for the conditions under which
it occurs.
